# Timing and cooking evidence

These are reviewable design estimates, not stopwatch measurements. Oven preheating is assumed to take 10–12 minutes and overlaps prep; actual ovens differ. Each recipe uses the stated convenience ingredients and food geometry. All estimates include loading, terminal checks, and finishing. Family dishes include heating one ready starch during cooking. Safe endpoints override clocks.

## Pressure exception is material

The named [Duo V5 manual](https://p.widencdn.net/bbdh9b/113-0059-01_110-0043-01_112-0170-01_113-0011-01_112-0010-01_110-0003-01_IB_DuoV5_MANUAL_399-1235-03US_US_R42-FINAL-09-30-2025_Lo.pdf), linked from the [manufacturer manual index](https://instantpot.com/pages/multi-cooker-product-manuals), requires 375 mL thin liquid for 6 QT, puts the cook countdown after pressure building, and allows preheating to take up to 30 minutes. It also requires waiting for the float valve to drop before opening. Thus 40–45 is an expected planning estimate, not a hard upper bound. With 30-minute pressure building the same recipe can take 59 minutes. This limitation is in the recipe; a firm deadline calls for an oven or air fryer choice.

[Instant Pot's Chicken Thighs with Island Salsa](https://instantpot.com/blogs/recipes/chicken-thighs-with-island-salsa) uses fresh boneless thighs without browning, a seven-minute pressure phase, a 165°F check, and salsa after cooking. It supports the mechanism by analogy. Its water volume and headline time do not override the current manual or validate this custom 8+10 method. No exact manufacturer test of this recipe was found. Shreddability at the proposed endpoint is a first-cook test priority.

The original dried-lentil soup was removed because full natural release produced a 40–65-minute published estimate with a plausible longer tail. No quick-release shortcut was used to force it under the timing bar.

## Sources and adaptation boundaries

- [FoodSafety.gov safe temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures) supports chicken/turkey 165°F, fish 145°F, meat-free eggs 160°F, and reheating 165°F. It does not validate the recipe timer.
- [USDA chicken handling](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/chicken-farm-table) supports separating raw poultry from ready-to-eat food and avoiding washing raw poultry.
- [Instant Pot salmon/asparagus recipe](https://instantpot.com/blogs/recipes/super-easy-roast-salmon-and-asparagus) supports pairing fish and asparagus in an air-frying/roasting appliance. Our two-fryer batch and temperature are adaptations, not an exact tested transfer.
- [McCormick roasted vegetables](https://www.mccormick.com/blogs/recipes/easy-roasted-vegetables) supports single-layer roasting and explicit pan geometry. Cod and zucchini are separated to avoid combined wet loading.
- [Cuisinart sheet-pan fajitas](https://www.cuisinart.com/recipes/entrees/sheetpan_fajitas.html) and [AdventHealth's original culinary recipe](https://www.uchicagomedicineadventhealth.org/recipes/easy-weeknight-sheet-pan-chicken-fajitas) support cut-poultry/fresh-pepper oven cooking. Their cut, convection/broil method, and timings differ; only the general mechanism transfers.
- [DeLallo's sheet-pan pesto gnocchi](https://www.delallo.com/recipe/sheet-pan-pesto-gnocchi/) supports roasting its shelf-stable gnocchi without boiling and using canned lentils in a chicken tray meal. Our broccoli/white-bean timing and the delayed beans are original design changes.
- [ABC's original chicken-lentil tray bake](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-02/easy-chicken-and-lentil-tray-bake/104313206) supports the chicken/lentil pairing and a specified shallow tray. It uses raw meat and a different cooking sequence, so it does not prove our cooked-chicken reheating time.
- [Campbell's original moist cooked-chicken bake](https://www.campbells.com/pace/recipes/mini-cheesy-chicken-enchiladas/) supports heating already-cooked chicken in a moist sauce in shallow portions. Its geometry differs; 165°F testing is still required for our covered lentil dish.
- [Potatoes USA microwave recipe guidance](https://potatogoodness.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Wow_Book_For_Web_Potatoes_USA_Branding.pdf) supports microwaving as a potato preparation shortcut. Our 567 g/¾-inch/1000 W 6–8-minute stage is an untested estimate with a fork-tender endpoint, not a copied manufacturer-tested formula.

Taste and texture mechanisms are design judgments: acid after cooking, savory seasonings, measured oil, room for roasting, pesto after heat, full yogurt with spiced legumes, foil for a moist precooked-chicken bake, beans added later to limit drying, and seeds used sparingly or as a binder. No claim that anyone tasted these meals is made.

## First-cook priorities

1. Time the pressure chicken from loading to plating, including full venting and shredding; confirm its tenderness and whether this household's cooker exceeds the expected window.
2. Probe the center of the covered cooked-chicken/lentil bake at 20 and 25 minutes; log actual depth and starting temperature. Test the frittata's spinach drain, center set, and final temperature.
3. Fit the measured fish, broccoli, and patties in the actual baskets before deciding the primary method; compare the oven fallback when fit fails.
4. Check two-rack browning, potato head-start timing, gnocchi centers, and white-bean drying. Taste harissa/yogurt balance and seed amounts before repeating the recipe.

These kitchen checks are intentionally unperformed. The documentary/design bar does not equate to kitchen validation.
