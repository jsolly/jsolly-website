# Meal gauntlet charter

Goal: deliver an evidence-backed replacement for the blog post, usable by a household with two air fryers, two Instant Pots, a conventional oven, and both keto and non-keto diners.

Mode: implementation. The recipes and their practical execution are the product. A publication package is the destination; publishing to the migration repository is separate.

Find-the-bar mandate: the user requested sustained, diligent gauntlet review but supplied no numeric acceptance thresholds. The lead sets and investigates the thresholds below as operational definitions of fast, low-attention, nutrient-dense meals. These are not claimed to be user-supplied dietary requirements.

## Acceptance bar

1. Three clearly separated groups: keto, shared keto main with a non-keto family add-on, and non-keto. No fixed recipe count is required if replacing or removing a weak choice improves the collection.
2. Every recipe has a four-serving yield, measured ingredients with unambiguous food state and cut size, appliance capacity/layout, temperature or pressure setting, sequence, doneness endpoint, and finishing instructions. Ingredient use must reconcile with the method.
3. Fast means no more than 15 minutes of estimated total hands-on work, including prep, loading, interventions, sauces, and finishing. Aim for at most 40 minutes from an ordinary start with specified convenience ingredients to dinner; up to 45 minutes is allowed only when explicitly identified and the scheduling arithmetic supports it. Preheating, pressurization, release, and family add-ons cannot be hidden. Thawing may be a stated prerequisite, not silently included.
4. Low watching means no initial sauté, overnight marinade, stovetop browning, reduction, continuous stirring, or raw dried-bean preparation. At most two brief mid-cook interventions. State start/stop timers and terminal temperature checks honestly. Appliance switching, shaking, flipping, and shredding all count as active work. Have an oven fallback when air fryer capacity is unrealistic.
5. Flavor has a defensible mechanism: appropriate seasoning, acid or fresh finishing, suitable fat, and texture management. Avoid unseasoned sides, overcooked greens, crowded trays, watery fish/vegetables, or obligatory seed additions that undermine taste. Taste cannot be proven without cooking; label recipes untested and avoid fabricated experience.
6. Nutrient density has inspectable composition: approximately 25 g or more protein per adult serving and at least 100 g non-starchy vegetables per serving. If a recipe falls slightly short of a numeric threshold, improve it rather than handwave. Calories are shown to reveal light dinners; portions are not prescribed energy needs. Approximate calories, protein, total carbs, fiber, and net carbs must come from traceable USDA or manufacturer data with weights, raw/cooked state, sauce/seed inclusion, and uncertainty recorded. No false precision or guaranteed ketosis claim.
7. Keto and shared keto bases target no more than 10 g estimated net carbs per serving, including vegetables, sauces, and specified boosters. Sauce brand limits and variable portions must be explicit. Non-keto add-ons are measured per person, separate from the common main, easy to heat, and add at most a few minutes active work. Include the estimate for the add-on or its label-based carb contribution.
8. Safety and cooking mechanics use current primary sources. Thin-liquid and fill limits must match a named pressure-cooker assumption while deferring to the actual model's manual. Lentil foam, frozen food, raw-poultry cross-contamination, and reheating issues must be addressed where they arise. A timer alone is not a safety endpoint.
9. The collection should be meaningfully varied, prioritize the user's appliances, reuse reasonable grocery staples, and use flax, hemp, and nutritional yeast where they improve a dish. Do not force every booster into every dinner.
10. The saved Markdown, HTML, and JSON patch agree; preserve the old URL. Overview links, serving counts, and timing/nutrition rows match the full recipes. Desktop and mobile local preview must be readable with working anchors/table scrolling and no page errors. Migrated-app rendering and kitchen tests remain separately reported limits.

## Independent pieces

- Piece A: keto recipes and their dinner completeness, macros, taste, and appliance execution.
- Piece B: family-convertible mains, separation of starches, complete cooking schedules, macros, and ease.
- Piece C: non-keto recipes, plant-protein completeness, fast/low-attention execution, pressure mechanics, and taste.
- Lead: reproducible nutrition calculations, evidence dossier, timing arithmetic, source/HTML/JSON consistency, and preview verification. Final independent review also checks these common materials and cross-category consistency.

## Critic output

Judge real artifacts against the bar. Return bar met or specific failures with recipe/line evidence, severity, and a feasible correction or useful test. Distinguish a correctable design gap from a kitchen-only uncertainty. Do not require tasting or kitchen tests to call the documentary/design bar met, but require honest disclosure and useful test priorities. Do not expand scope with optional improvements. Fresh final review must inspect the revised artifact and calculation evidence, not builder summaries.
